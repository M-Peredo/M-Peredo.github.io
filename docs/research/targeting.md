# Targeting

Who the site is built for. These are the inputs that tailor the Phase 1 market research, and every later decision about copy and case studies should check against them.

*Captured 2026-10-02, from Molly via Carlos.*

## Roles
- **Level:** mid-career to senior individual contributor (IC). Titles with "Senior" or "Lead."
- **Not management.** She wants to be the primary lead and expert on projects end to end, not a people manager.
- **Titles:** Product Designer first; UX Designer very much in play. She calls herself a product designer, but UX is a huge part of her role.
- **Research:** not a dedicated researcher, though she leads a lot of user interviews.
- She may apply to lower-level roles, but the site isn't designed for them.

## Domain
- **K-12 ed tech** is the primary target: 10+ years of K-12 experience.
- **Higher ed:** open to it.
- **Workforce learning:** may apply, but the site isn't tailored to it.
- **Nonprofits:** not the target, and somewhat dispreferred, even though her current role is at one (Code.org).

## Strengths to lead with
- **AI-driven design work is her major strength.** She works almost entirely AI-driven now.
- Case studies will be large: platform-wide AI adoption initiatives, teacher platform tools, and similar.

## Logistics
- Remote only. This doesn't change the research.

## Implications for the research
- Research ed tech product companies (K-12 first, higher ed second), not nonprofits or general tech.
- Focus on senior/lead IC product and UX design roles: what reviewers expect from someone who owns projects end to end.
- AI expectations are central, not a side topic: how senior designers are expected to show AI-driven work, and how to show it credibly when it's her main strength.
- Case study selection is decided in the case study sessions, not in the research.
